# Surprise Rail — Brand Strategy

**Project** Surprise Rail — a streaming discovery concept that reduces decision paralysis by
withholding cover art and offering a short clue instead.
**Author** Pranavi Ram. Concept, hidden and revealed states, clue language, prototype.
**Context** Developed during a product design internship at HBO Max.
**Status** Speculative concept. Not shipped, not an official HBO Max campaign, not endorsed by
HBO Max. Everything in this folder is portfolio work authored by Pranavi.

---

## 1. The audit in one paragraph

Streaming interfaces compete by showing more. More rows, more art, more confident
recommendations. The result is a wall of certainty that is exhausting to evaluate, and people
scroll past things they would have liked because recognising a title is not the same as wanting
it. Surprise Rail withholds the art and gives you two or three words instead — *Dystopian Wars*,
*Suburban Secrets*, *NYC Punk Dream* — and bets that a small, deliberate gap in information
produces more want than a full picture does.

The critical design decision, and the thing the identity has to protect: **a blur reads as a
broken image.** Pranavi tested occlusion treatments and found that anything that looks like a
loading failure destroys the mechanic. So the hidden state became a **film-strip frame** —
sprocket perforations top and bottom, a frosted near-white window, the clue set in dark type
inside it. It sits inline with real cover art without pretending to be cover art. You can tell at
a glance that something is being kept from you *on purpose*.

That tile is the single most important visual fact in the project. The identity is built out of
it, not over it.

## 2. Three territories

### Territory A — PERFORATION

The sprocket hole becomes the atomic unit of the whole system. A perforation is four things at
once: a hole (missing information), a rhythm (a rail), the mechanism by which film physically
advances (the machinery of the reveal), and an aperture that lets light through. Every asset is
framed by a perforated band; the spacing grid is the sprocket pitch; the wordmark is masked by
the perforation row so only slices of the letters show. Type is tight uppercase display against
mono metadata. Motion advances frame by frame with a deliberate stepped stutter before locking
into register. Cinematic, mechanical, premium, unmistakably specific to this product and to no
other streaming feature.
**Risk:** analogue-film nostalgia. Kodak, Super 8, grain, sepia. Must be kept precise and
digital — no texture, no distress, no vintage colour.

### Territory B — THE FROSTED WINDOW

Build on translucency. A defined focus ladder — five states from fully occluded to fully
resolved — governs every asset, and the wordmark itself exists across all five. The pale
lavender sheen from Pranavi's naming study becomes light passing through frosted glass. Soft,
atmospheric, and the closest to existing brand equity.
**Risk, and it is fatal:** this is glassmorphism, and blur is the exact treatment Pranavi's own
testing rejected. A brand built on softened obstruction contradicts the research finding that
made the product work. The territory argues against its own case study.

### Territory C — THE CLUE

The descriptor is the entire identity. No graphic device at all. Campaign assets are nothing but
two-word clues set enormous in a pearl-to-spectral ramp on ink — the *absence* of imagery is the
brand. Confident, editorial, infinitely scalable, cheap to produce, and it foregrounds the
verbal system Pranavi actually wrote and tested.
**Risk:** nothing is proprietary. Any brand can set big type on black. It has no relationship to
the film-strip tile, so the identity and the product would look like two separate projects — the
precise failure a portfolio case study cannot afford.

---

## 3. Chosen direction — **REGISTER**

**Territory A, with Territory C's verbal system absorbed into it, and Territory B's spectral
sheen retained as light rather than blur.**

### Why

*Registration* is the real mechanical process of aligning a film frame precisely in the gate so
the image sits sharp and steady. Out of register, the frame is misaligned and unreadable. In
register, it resolves. That is the product, described in the language of the medium it lives in.
It is not a metaphor imported from outside — it is what Pranavi's tile already is, named
correctly.

It also solves the three problems the identity has to solve at once:

- It **extends** the film-strip tile instead of competing with it. The tile is the product; the
  gate is the brand. Same geometry, different scale.
- It gives concealment a **mechanism** rather than an effect. Hiding is something the system
  *does* (a frame not yet advanced), not something applied on top (a blur). That is the
  difference between an authored device and a filter.
- It gives motion a **reason**. Everything in the system moves the way film moves: advance,
  stutter, lock. Nothing eases for decoration.

### The tradeoff, honestly

REGISTER is narrower than Territory C. It commits hard to a cinematic frame of reference, which
means it would not stretch to a music or podcast surface without rework, and it demands more
production discipline — perforations drawn wrong, or textured, and it collapses into retro
pastiche. I am choosing specificity over stretch. A portfolio case study is better served by an
identity that could only belong to this project than by one that could go anywhere.

### Signature device — **the gate**

A frame bounded by two rows of perforations. Anything can sit inside it: a clue, a still, a
research chart, a headline, nothing at all. Its state tells you where you are in the mechanic.
Occluded gate, clue gate, in-register gate. One shape, three meanings.

### Signature moment

A strip of frames advancing horizontally past a fixed gate. Each frame that lands in register
resolves from clue to title, and the brass register mark strikes on the lock. This anchors the
case study hero, the motion loop, the posters, and the transitions between chapters.

## 4. Positioning

**For** people who open a streaming app with time to spend and no title in mind,
**Surprise Rail is** a discovery row that withholds the cover art and offers a clue,
**so that** choosing becomes an act of curiosity instead of an act of evaluation.
**Unlike** recommendation rails, which compete by showing more and asking you to judge faster,
**Surprise Rail** removes information on purpose and makes the gap itself the invitation.

**Category** streaming discovery / entertainment product concept.
**Compared against** the algorithmic hero row, the Continue Watching rail, endless-scroll
browsing, and the offline behaviour it is actually modelled on — a blind-date-with-a-book
display, where the book is wrapped in paper printed with only a few descriptive words.

**Pranavi's contribution, stated plainly**
Framed decision paralysis as the product opportunity. Developed the concept end to end. Designed
the hidden and revealed states, including the testing that rejected blur and produced the
film-strip frame. Wrote and tested the teaser clue language. Ran the descriptor research that
sorted clue types into atmospheric, thematic, tactical and behavioural, and mapped their impact
on decision-making. Prototyped the rail across streaming surfaces.

**Proof available** the descriptor research map, the hidden-state testing sheet, the naming
study, the working CTV prototype, and the clue set itself.

## 5. Brand promise

**You get less, and it is on purpose.**

Everything in the system has to make withholding read as generosity — an invitation, not a
withheld answer. The moment concealment reads as a paywall, an error, or a trick, the brand has
failed.

## 6. Core tension

**Between seeing and knowing.** A recommendation shows you everything and tells you nothing about
why you would want it. Surprise Rail shows you almost nothing and tells you exactly enough. The
identity lives in the gap and refuses to close it early.

## 7. Personality

| Is | Is not |
| --- | --- |
| Cinematic — the language of frames, gates, projection | Nostalgic. No grain, no sepia, no distress |
| Editorial — a point of view, set in type | Decorative |
| Withholding, warmly | Coy, teasing, or cute |
| Precise — engineered spacing, mechanical rhythm | Clinical |
| Slightly surreal — objects behave like film, not like UI | Whimsical |
| Confident enough to say nothing | Loud |

## 8. Design principles

1. **Concealment must look engineered.** If a hidden state could be mistaken for a loading
   failure, redraw it. This is a research finding, not a preference.
2. **The gate is the only frame.** No cards, no rounded containers, no drop shadows. Content is
   either in a gate or on the ground.
3. **Space on the pitch.** All spacing derives from the sprocket pitch. Ad-hoc padding is how
   this system stops looking machined.
4. **Light passes, it does not glow.** The spectral ramp is a projector beam through a frosted
   frame. It has a source and a direction. No ambient bloom, no atmospheric haze as filler.
5. **Brass marks register, and nothing else.** One warm accent, one meaning: *this frame is in
   register.* Below 2% of any surface.
6. **Asymmetry by default.** Frames run off the edge because film is continuous. A centred,
   fully contained composition should be the exception and should be earned.
7. **Never fake the content.** Revealed states use marked placeholder plates. No invented cover
   art, no fabricated titles, no invented metrics.
8. **The official HBO Max logo is never altered, restyled, or absorbed into the system.** HBO Max
   is the context this concept was designed inside; it is not the author and not a brand partner
   here.

## 9. Verbal principles

The clue *is* the verbal identity. The rules Pranavi wrote for clues govern all campaign copy.

- **Two or three words.** Five at absolute most, and only in a headline.
- **Name the feeling or the world, never the plot.** *Suburban Secrets*, not *a family hides a
  crime*.
- **No title words.** A clue that leaks the name has failed.
- **No instruction verbs.** Never *discover*, *explore*, *dive in*, *unlock*.
- **Concrete nouns beat adjectives.** *Dance Floor Disaster* works. *Emotional Journey* does not.
- **Withhold, then stop.** No follow-up sentence explaining the withholding. The gap does the
  work.

**Words to use:** frame, gate, register, advance, clue, hidden, withheld, resolve, cut, still,
reel, on purpose.
**Words never to use:** immersive, seamless, bold, innovative, curated, elevate, reimagined,
unlock, dive in, next-generation.

**Voice check —** *knowing, not knowing-better.* The brand has seen the film and is not going to
tell you. It is amused about it, but it is not smug.

> Yes — "Two words. That is the whole offer."
> No — "Get ready to discover your next obsession."

## 10. Taglines

### Primary

# Look before you know.

Four words. It inverts the phrase everyone already carries — *know before you look* — and by
inverting it, states the product mechanic exactly: you are given sight before you are given
knowledge, and the gap between them is the invitation. It works set 200pt across a poster and it
works at 11px in a mono metadata line. It is an invitation rather than a withholding, which is
the specific note chapter 05 of the case study asks for. And it beats the incumbent *See less.
Want more.* — that line is a symmetric less/more advertising construction that could sit under
any minimalist product; this one could not.

### Alternates

1. **Two words. Then decide.** — the mechanic, literally. Best for product surfaces and the App
   Store style feature line.
2. **Kept from you, on purpose.** — for the hidden-state and research assets. Says the design
   decision out loud. Colder; use where confidence matters more than warmth.
3. **Every frame starts hidden.** — system line. Best on the strip and storyboard assets, where
   the rule is being demonstrated rather than sold.
4. **Now showing: not yet.** — the cinema-marquee joke. For social and out-of-home only; too
   arch for the product.
5. **See less. Want more.** — retained incumbent. Still the sharpest short line for a rail
   header inside the app, where four syllables is the whole budget.

---

## 11. What the identity must not do

It must not redraw, restyle, or "improve" the film-strip tile. That artefact is the product and
it is already correct. The identity extends its geometry outward to poster, motion and campaign
scale, and stops at the edge of the interface.
